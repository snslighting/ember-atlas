Accuracy & detection limits

Can it detect a burning house?

A small, hot fire can produce a signal within a larger pixel, but detecting every house fire is not reliable. Heat intensity, duration, viewing angle and clear skies matter. These satellites cannot identify which building is burning.

How much detail is available?

VIIRS pixels are nominally 375 m and MODIS pixels about 1 km near nadir. Pixels grow with viewing geometry; source scan × track dimensions are shown in activity details when available. Pixel size is neither a minimum fire size nor a location-error estimate. Zooming the basemap adds visual detail, not thermal resolution.

What can be missed or misidentified?

Clouds, heavy smoke and tree canopy can obscure fires. Short fires may occur between overpasses; small or cool fires may be missed. Industrial heat, gas flares and other thermal anomalies can resemble fire signals. No detection does not prove there is no fire or that a fire has ended.

How accurate is an attention alert?

No validated fire-detection accuracy percentage is available for Ember Atlas. NASA confidence describes detection quality; it is not a probability that a building is burning. Our evidence labels and seasonal percentiles are not wildfire probabilities: the 100th percentile means unusually high activity compared with the archive, not 100% certainty of a fire. Groups can combine adjacent activities; support cells are not burned area. Comparable seasonal history currently covers only four prepared archives.

Is this continuous live surveillance?

The page checks for updates every 60 seconds. New observations depend on satellite overpasses and NASA processing, so a fresh download can contain older observations. Check the acquisition time on each activity.
