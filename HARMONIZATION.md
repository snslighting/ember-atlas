# Ember Atlas harmonization methodology

## What the existing pipeline did

`fetch-firms.js` downloads NASA rolling MODIS C6.1 and NOAA-20 VIIRS C2 global CSVs, retains five UTC dates, parses columns through `core.parseCSV`, normalizes invalid FRP, hashes records and publishes daily sensor shards. `provider.js`/`analysis-worker.js`/`map-analysis.js` select raw rows by AOI, UTC date and a numeric UI quality score. Previously `core.harmonize` assigned each point to a latitude-adjusted approximately 1 km daily grid, averaged positions, kept maximum mapped confidence and maximum FRP, and counted each cell once. Multiple overpasses and sensors shared one cell. It did not estimate a true fire location or account for the satellite footprint.

`fetch-history.js` saves historical SP/NRT CSVs and checksums in `.history-source`. `build-history.js` parses and verifies them, applies MODIS >=40 and VIIRS nominal/high thresholds, deduplicates rows, filters the prepared country/AOI and creates center-assigned daily cells and source-specific contributions. `history-core.dailyStatistics` constructs daily occupied cells by source, retaining unavailable products as null and downloaded days without detections as zero. `fitCalibration` originally fit one sum(MODIS)/sum(VIIRS) ratio through 2020 for each region/product; `activity` averaged observed MODIS with a selected scaled VIIRS count. Matching calendar dates in other years supply seasonal percentiles. NRT never trains this model. These historical cell files preserve aggregated contributions, not recoverable individual observations; raw CSVs are therefore required for temporal intercomparison.

## Products implemented

1. **Raw FIRMS detections** remain unchanged in sensor/compare views and source files. Native `confidenceRaw`, product, satellite, acquisition UTC, scan/track, processing collection, FRP and source checksum must remain available. Numeric VIIRS 30/70/95 values remain only legacy display filter scores. Native quality categories are also attached to derived products; no calibrated probability is inferred.
2. **Live detection-support grid (`evidence-grid-v2`)** deduplicates identical source records; distributes each observation over intersecting grid cells using normalized approximate footprint intersections rather than averaging point coordinates. Dimensions use scan/track in km, with 1 km MODIS and .375 km VIIRS fallbacks. Coordinates are fixed support cell centers. All source IDs, source-product/satellite/daynight contributions, acquisition-time range, evidence weights, native quality classes and explicit uncertainty are attached. Weights sum to one per source detection, not one per fire. Cell counts represent support cells, and source observations can contribute to more than one cell. This is deliberately a different quantity from historical center-cell counts. The retained scalar FRP is maximum positive single-pixel FRP, explicitly labeled; zero is unavailable for this statistic, not a measurement of zero fire power. No cross-sensor or across-time FRP sum is made.
3. **Cross-sensor spatial-temporal associations** (`matchSensors`) require UTC time separation <=15 minutes, matching known day/night state, and intersecting approximate footprint bounds with a .25 km margin. Association is many-to-many because MODIS and VIIRS have different footprints. Tests include adjacent UTC dates and longitude wrap. 5/30 minute alternatives quantify threshold sensitivity. Associations are validation diagnostics, not merged events or calibrated detection probabilities. Do not interpret sensor absence as clear-sky non-fire. Exact duplicates are removed; overlapping but nonidentical swath detections cannot safely be deduplicated without granule/pixel metadata.
4. **Seasonal regional MODIS-equivalent activity** (`fitSeasonalCalibration`) uses the established historical daily center-cell counts so existing historical datasets remain comparable. For each selected AOI and product, fit a regional paired-SP ratio through 2020, requiring 30 paired days, three training years and positive totals. Fit monthly ratios where there are >=30 days and >=3 years; shrink each toward the regional ratio with weight n/(n+60). Sparse months fall back explicitly. No coefficient is learned from holdout years or NRT. Delete-one-training-year monthly ratio ranges report model stability, not confidence intervals. Current historical context applies coefficients date by date, including ranges crossing calendar months. No region-specific coefficient is applied to the global live grid.

Historical calibration and live footprint support are intentionally distinct derived products. Footprint support does not turn VIIRS pixel counts into MODIS counts. Nor can spatial-temporal matching alone remove sensitivity differences, cloud cover or diurnal fire variability. The seasonal model remains an empirical activity index, not a probability of an ongoing fire or a estimate of burned area. Raw spatial-temporal pairs are diagnostics of the statistical model's assumptions, not its training labels.

## Scientific basis and constraints

- NASA's [MODIS C6/C6.1 guide](https://modis-fire.umd.edu/guides.html) documents nominal 1 km fire pixels, confidence quality and FRP. Scan/track dimensions grow away from nadir; a point marks a detected pixel rather than precise fire location. We use reported dimensions, but FIRMS CSV does not supply orientation or exact corners. The latitude-adjusted grid is approximate, not a globally exact equal-area projection. Above 85 degrees latitude the code flags a center fallback. AOI clipping currently selects source centers before allocating footprints, creating an edge selection effect. A globally equal-area, exact-swath implementation needs richer Level-2 geometry.
- NASA's [VIIRS Collection 2 375 m guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2024-07/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) explains confidence classes and FRP retrieval. VIIRS 375 m detections use coarser M13 radiance; multiple 375 m fire pixels can share fractional FRP. Saturation/background retrieval problems may produce zero/null FRP. Parent M13 pixel identifiers are missing from CSV; invented reconstruction or FRP amplification would be unsupported. High confidence is not automatically greater FRP precision.
- [Schroeder et al. (2014), doi:10.1016/j.rse.2013.12.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2013.12.008) establishes sensitivity and mapping differences of the VIIRS 375 m product. Its reported accuracy in specific assessments is not transferable to this app or every location. Nominal spatial resolutions cannot justify equating counts or combining confidence numerically.
- [Li et al. (2020), doi:10.3390/rs12101561](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12101561) investigates MODIS-missed FRP using VIIRS across Africa. Contemporary acquisition, view geometry and small-fire sensitivity matter; a count ratio is not a universal physical correction. The code exposes temporal and FRP-stratified consistency, rather than treating unmatched pixels as truth. Its 15-minute tolerance and .25 km margin are engineering assumptions tested for sensitivity, not constants prescribed by this paper.
- NASA's [confidence explanation](https://forum.earthdata.nasa.gov/viewtopic.php?t=4541) states confidence categories are not statistical Type I/II confidence levels. The UI score cannot be interpreted as percent probability.
- NASA's [VIIRS active fire product description](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/Products/NASA/FireESDR.html) describes recurring polar-orbiter sampling. Fires change between overpasses. Terra/Aqua and individual VIIRS platforms remain separate provenance; regional daily count calibration still mixes Terra/Aqua and therefore retains sampling bias. Product downloads are not proof of clear-sky exposure.

Full PDFs were not all retrievable through the browsing service; source indexing and NASA product documentation supplied the accessible evidence. Further scientific review should consult full ATBDs and Level-2 data. No unpublished numerical correction factor from a paper has been copied into this model.

## Validation and reproducibility

Run `npm test`, `npm run harmonization:validate`, `npm run data:refresh`, and `npm run build`. Validation uses cached original checksummed NASA files, all SP overlap years 2017–2024 available in the three prepared regions, full seasonal Uzbekistan records and August/September regional cases. Historical ratio calibration uses earlier SP records through 2020; years 2021 onward are held out. `validation/HARMONIZATION-VALIDATION.md` contains observed RMSE/MAE/bias versus MODIS and `validation/harmonization-results.json` provides hashes, matching sensitivity, source quality/FRP availability and evidence conservation. Current NRT data are a separate pipeline check, never calibration labels. Source data are NASA-derived rather than synthetic; unit fixtures test edge cases only.

A lower held-out RMSE indicates consistency with a MODIS reference, not absolute fire accuracy. Report worsening results, sparse strata and sensitivity. No ground-truth omission/commission rates, recall, precision, calibrated probabilities, operational fire-response skill or perfection are established. More defensible physical calibration would require Level-2 fire/cloud/non-fire masks, exact geolocation and view angles, matched Aqua/VIIRS clear-sky opportunities, product-specific radiometry, independent high-resolution fire reference, biome/land-use stratification and repeated prospective validation. Prepared location/month baselines must not transfer automatically to other locations.


Supplemental validation downloads 24 national NASA MODIS/S-NPP annual CSV files (Kenya and Australia, 2018–2023), retains full source files and checksums, then selects the declared geographic bounds. It compares train 2018–2020 with holdout 2021–2023. Independent NIFC incident perimeters for northern California 2017–2019 provide retrospective same-year point/center/footprint spatial-consistency diagnostics. These are different estimands and are not fire precision/recall. See validation/SUPPLEMENTAL-VALIDATION.md. The cached rerun option verifies downloaded checksums; ordinary runs download again.
