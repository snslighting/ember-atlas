THE EVIDENCE BEHIND THE VIEW

Connected observations.
Transparent assumptions.

Live observations and historical archives share an approximate 1 km daily grid. History adds a transparent overlap-era calibration and compares activity with matching seasonal periods. This is a prototype baseline, not a validated fire-risk product.

01 / REAL SOURCES

A record across sensor eras.

Live covers the world using MODIS_NRT and VIIRS_NOAA20_NRT. History uses NASA FIRMS Standard Processing where available: MODIS from November 2000, S-NPP VIIRS from January 2012, and NOAA-20 VIIRS from April 2018. NOAA-21 NRT begins in January 2024; this build has no NOAA-21 Standard Processing series. Products are never assumed to exist before their availability.

02 / PREPROCESS

Small partitions. Full provenance.

Offline processing validates dates, coordinates and source checksums, removes duplicate observation rows, and retains daily cell IDs, sensor counts, acquisition-time bounds, satellites, products and maximum nonnegative FRP. Historical maps load year/geographic partitions; summaries drive the calendar. The private NASA key stays outside browser assets.

03 / QUALITY

Confidence is not a probability.

The historical baseline keeps MODIS native confidence ≥40 and VIIRS nominal/high detections. Low/nominal/high are represented as 30/70/95 solely for filtering. Live has an independent display confidence control; its historical comparison always applies the fixed historical rules. Maximum FRP is not summed energy. Missing or negative reported FRP is unavailable.

04 / GRID

One cell per place and UTC day.

Latitude rows are approximately 1 km high. Longitude spacing adjusts by the cosine of each row center. An occupied cell is counted once per product/day, with all quality-filtered source counts attached. Historical coordinates are grid centers; Live distributes each detection over an approximate scan/track footprint and uses fixed grid centers. Source IDs, fractional evidence weights, native quality classes and uncertainty remain attached. These cells describe detection support, not fire locations. Country/AOI filtering uses generalized borders; historical boundary selection by cell center can differ at edges.

05 / CALIBRATE

A seasonal overlap-era model.

For each VIIRS product, k = Σ MODIS occupied cells / Σ VIIRS occupied cells across paired days for the same area, using Standard Processing through 2020. Both product files must cover a day, including zero-detection days. At least 30 paired days, three years, and positive totals are required. Each selected AOI gets its own regional factor. Month-specific ratios are shrunk toward it with a 60-day prior strength; sparse months fall back to the regional ratio. Year-deletion ranges describe stability, not probability. NRT never trains the model. Spatial-temporal association diagnostics use a 15-minute window and approximate footprint overlap; calibration still targets daily occupied-cell consistency.

06 / COMBINE

A MODIS-scale activity index.

For each day: H = (M + kV) / 2 when both calibrated contributions are available; use M or kV if only one is available; otherwise show missing coverage. Reference VIIRS is S-NPP, then NOAA-20, then NOAA-21 when calibrated. We do not add overlapping VIIRS satellites together. Monthly H sums daily values. H is an occupied-cell activity proxy with no 0–100 cap; it is not burned area, fire severity, or probability.

07 / CHECK

Report held-out disagreement.

Standard Processing after 2020 is held out from fitting. Report daily RMSE and mean bias of kV minus M, plus training and holdout sample sizes. Earlier periods are in-sample. These diagnostics measure disagreement, not truth or scientific accuracy. No correction for cloud cover, clear-sky sampling, orbit changes, overpass effort or spatially varying sensitivity is made.

08 / CONTEXT

Compare the same season.

For a selected month, compare H with the same calendar dates in other archived years for the selected area. Exclude the selected year and all provisional NRT reference periods. Require complete coverage of the selected date set in each reference year. Partial current months use matching partial dates, not complete months. Report the median, ratio to median, midrank percentile and sample z-score. At least five comparison years are required.

09 / THRESHOLDS

Activity labels with explicit limits.

Percentile = 100 × (years below + 0.5 × years tied) / comparison years. Normal: below 80; Elevated: 80 to below 95; Unusual: 95 or higher. Critical activity requires percentile ≥99 and at least 20 comparison years. These are relative activity labels, not emergency alerts. A zero median gives no ratio; zero variance gives no z-score.

10 / COVERAGE

Show what is actually available.

Uzbekistan covers November 2000 through the current record, with Standard Processing through June 2026 and subsequent NRT shown as provisional. Northern California covers September 2017–2024; the Rondônia Amazon case covers August 2017–2024. Current NRT can be appended to each case. Other historical months and places are not populated and remain blank. File availability is not proof of clear-sky observation; zero detections are not proof of no fire.

Fresh observations. An honest clock.

Our publisher is scheduled to fetch NASA observations every 15 minutes. The observatory checks for newly published data every 60 seconds and updates in place, preserving your view. GitHub's scheduler and deployment can be delayed. NASA near-real-time records also have acquisition and processing latency; this is not a continuous, instantaneous fire feed.

The dashboard displays retrieval age, last check, and stale or failed-update states. If an update fails, the last actual observations remain visible. Synthetic records are never substituted.

A thermal anomaly is not always a wildfire.

Thermal anomalies may represent agricultural burning, industrial activity or other heat sources. Counts are not unique fire events or burned area. Grid alignment plus a ratio calibration addresses a limited part of sensor differences. Held-out sensor consistency is evaluated locally; this does not establish fire-risk accuracy or suitability for operational response. Detection-only CSVs do not supply clear-sky non-fire exposure.

Read the NASA FIRMS API documentation, NASA latency information, and source code and analysis.

Sources: NASA FIRMS archive · Product availability · Area API. Historical builds retain retrieval time, original source links and checksums.

Map imagery has its own clock.

OpenStreetMap street and dark maps, EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless 2025 imagery with 10 m source detail, NASA Blue Marble imagery, VIIRS nighttime lights from 2012, and dated daily MODIS true-color imagery are separate from the automatically updated FIRMS observations. Sentinel-2, night lights and Blue Marble are dated composites. Daily imagery may contain clouds or coverage gaps; it is not a continuous live satellite stream. Display clusters combine nearby symbols to keep the world map responsive; filtering, metrics and CSV exports retain every selected record.

Country and region filters use generalized Natural Earth polygons. The Baikonur lease is grouped under Kazakhstan and included in its observation totals; the lease remains selectable within Kazakhstan.

MONITORING PRIORITIES

History gives today’s observations context.

Activity events group adjacent observed footprint support within 15-minute acquisition bins and link overlapping or adjacent support over gaps up to 24 hours. These groups are not confirmed wildfires or exact perimeters. Fixed native quality rules, source IDs, uncertainty and merge/split lineage are retained.

Comparable seasonal baselines use Work’s footprint algorithm on real historical NASA sources. Previous-year Standard Processing reference seasons and Work’s seasonal calibration supply the median, quantiles and percentile. No NRT training or future-year data is used. Watch begins at the 80th percentile, Elevated at the 95th. Critical requires at least 20 prior years, the 99th percentile, separated repeated observations, multiple sensors, and recent evidence. Missing history and observation gaps remain explicit.

The Ember activity index is the empirical seasonal percentile, with persistence, growth, sensor quality and freshness shown independently. It is not a weighted risk score or a probability of fire. Approaching critical means a measured rise in percentile and historically rapid observed-support growth; it does not predict ignition.

Rules you can inspect

Watch begins at the 80th seasonal percentile; Elevated at the 95th. Critical also requires an extreme percentile, twenty comparable previous years, repeated activity spanning at least three hours, both sensors and recent observations. Prepared overlap-era archives usually cannot support that twenty-year requirement.

At least five earlier Standard Processing years are needed. Comparisons use the same location and calendar season. Missing coverage or untrained sensor scaling stays explicitly unavailable. A sustained unusual period needs at least two consecutive covered days above the 95th percentile.

The activity index is the seasonal percentile. Persistence, expansion, sensor evidence and freshness remain independent; no weighted fire-probability score is hidden behind it. These are prototype monitoring policies, not emergency-dispatch thresholds.
