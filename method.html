THE EVIDENCE BEHIND THE VIEW

Connected observations.
Transparent assumptions.

Our harmonization baseline brings two detection records into a shared daily grid. It is an exploratory tool, with every processing step in view.

01 / OBSERVE

Actual NASA records.

MODIS_NRT and VIIRS_NOAA20_NRT observations arrive through NASA FIRMS. We retain coordinates, UTC date and time, satellite, original confidence, fire radiative power, and product metadata. The feed covers the whole world. Region presets use bounding boxes, not administrative boundaries.

02 / NORMALIZE

Apply shared filters.

Both sensors use the same worldwide or regional selection and UTC date range. MODIS numeric confidence is retained; VIIRS low/nominal/high is mapped heuristically to 30/70/95. This is a convenience for filtering, not a calibrated probability.

03 / CONNECT

A common daily grid.

Group observations by an approximate latitude-adjusted 1 km row grid per UTC day. Retain sensor membership, observation count and time bounds. Coordinates use the mean; confidence and FRP use the maximum. FRP is not summed energy. Unavailable or negative reported FRP is displayed as unavailable, with the original value retained in the CSV export.

04 / EXPLORE

Patterns, with context.

Count daily occupied cells and flag activity above the selected window's mean + 1.5 standard deviations. The latest UTC day may be incomplete. A five-day record is insufficient for historical anomalies or seasonal conclusions.

Fresh observations. An honest clock.

Our publisher is scheduled to fetch NASA observations every 15 minutes. The observatory checks for newly published data every 60 seconds and updates in place, preserving your view. GitHub's scheduler and deployment can be delayed. NASA near-real-time records also have acquisition and processing latency; this is not a continuous, instantaneous fire feed.

The dashboard displays retrieval age, last check, and stale or failed-update states. If an update fails, the last actual observations remain visible. Synthetic records are never substituted.

A thermal anomaly is not always a wildfire.

Detections may represent agricultural burning or other heat sources. Observation counts are not unique fire events or burned area. Cloud cover, sensor sensitivity, spatial resolution, and overpass timing differ. This baseline does not correct for those differences or scientifically validate paired events. An empty cell is not proof of no fire.

Read the NASA FIRMS API documentation, NASA latency information, and source code and analysis.

Map imagery has its own clock.

OpenStreetMap street and dark maps, NASA Blue Marble imagery, VIIRS nighttime lights from 2012, and dated daily MODIS true-color imagery are separate from the automatically updated FIRMS observations. Night lights and Blue Marble are static composites. Daily imagery may contain clouds or coverage gaps; it is not a continuous live satellite stream. Display clusters combine nearby symbols to keep the world map responsive; filtering, metrics and CSV exports retain every selected record.
